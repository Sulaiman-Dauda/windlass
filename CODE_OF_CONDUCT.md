# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Criticise code, not people.

## What that means here

**Do:**

- Give people the benefit of the doubt. Most confusing questions come from missing context, not
  carelessness.
- Keep technical disagreement technical. "This has a race condition, here is the sequence" is useful;
  "this is amateur work" is not.
- Accept that maintainers say no. This project deliberately does less than its competitors, so a
  well-argued feature request can still be declined on scope.
- Remember that people are contributing time they did not have to give.

**Do not:**

- Harass anyone, publicly or privately.
- Make it personal — insults, demeaning remarks, or comments about someone's identity, background or
  ability.
- Post sexualised content, or anything else that would make a reasonable person uncomfortable in a
  professional setting.
- Publish anyone's private information without their permission.
- Report a security vulnerability in public. That endangers users. Use the
  [security policy](./SECURITY.md).

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, discussions and any other project space, and to
anyone representing the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to **abiodauda@gmail.com**. Reports are handled privately, and the reporter's
identity is not disclosed.

Maintainers may edit or delete comments, close issues and pull requests, or ban an account,
proportionate to the situation. Sustained or serious breaches lead to a permanent ban.

Maintainers are held to this standard too — more so, since they set the tone.
